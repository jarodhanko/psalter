The owner has selected MIT. [LICENSE](../LICENSE) contains the standard text with `Copyright (c) 2026 Jarod Hanko`; [LICENSE-SCOPE.md](../LICENSE-SCOPE.md) states its scope separately without changing the MIT terms.

MIT applies to original application and tooling code, tests, and associated original documentation. It does not relicense musical works, lyrics, notation/transcriptions/arrangements, KJV text, soundfonts, recordings, images, or other third-party assets. Embedded content in data modules, examples, and test fixtures keeps its own terms. Original code that processes that content remains MIT, regardless of directory or file extension. There is no blanket license for the complete music corpus.

Existing third-party licenses and public-domain status remain specific to their own components and editions. This change does not impose new rights over public-domain content or grant rights the project author does not hold. See the scope statement before assuming that every part of a mixed source file is MIT-covered.

This is a summary of recorded evidence and open release decisions, not a legal opinion or worldwide clearance. The 2026-10-13 follow-up research substantially narrows the earlier notice-based concerns: it has not established a rights issue requiring removal of Psalter nos. 1–434. Specific remaining evidence checks are identified below. Audit flags are not automatic exclusion decisions, and this preparation leaves the working application's content unchanged.

## Source publication and live delivery are separate

Withholding score source files from a public repository does not by itself resolve the rights question for the live application. A publicly reachable static build still sends scores, lyrics, and other bundled music assets to visitors' browsers, where they can be downloaded; installed/offline copies retain those assets too. Review the source export and the live application's delivery separately, against the exact editions and intended distribution scope.

The existence of other free Psalter applications is useful research context, but does not alone establish the rights basis for the exact text, harmony, edition, territory, and use here. It also does not prove that there is a rights problem. Verify the underlying evidence before deciding whether any change or exclusion is necessary. No new exclusion or live-content change is authorized by this document.

## Psalter: current evidence, not presumptive removal

**The follow-up review has not established that Psalter nos. 1–434 need removal.** The earlier description of 34 later settings as a single group of unresolved release blockers overstates the current evidence. The [existing rights audit](rights-audit.md) remains a useful historical notice inventory; use the distinctions and newer evidence below rather than treating its older labels as findings of current protection.

### Original 1912 material

The original U.S.-published 1912 material is beyond the maximum U.S. copyright term. This supports the original nos. 1–413 to the extent the exact text and harmony are present in that edition. Old printed copyright or permission wording does not keep that material protected after expiry. A later replacement text or harmony must be identified separately. See the [U.S. Copyright Office's duration guidance](https://copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf).

The 14 undated notices previously singled out—7, 63, 77, 78, 92, 168, 203, 219, 236, 240, 242, 322, 403, and 411—should remain as historical attribution/provenance. Their wording alone is not a reason to exclude an otherwise expired 1912 setting.

### 1934 settings, including nos. 414–432

The follow-up located and image-checked the Christian Reformed Church's original registration **A80029**, dated December 28, 1934, in the [1935 Music Catalog of Copyright Entries, page 65, entry 1660](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig303libr/page/65/mode/1up). This identifies a compilation registration and claimant, not necessarily every underlying text's separate registration or the scope of its new material. A bounded search of 1961–63 Music catalog OCR and Books renewal transcriptions found no matching registration, title, or claimant renewal. This supports non-renewal as a possibility; it is not a conclusive certificate of no renewal, and separately published 1931 texts require their own check. A visible 1934 notice alone does not establish that protection remains today.

The search covered six complete half-year Music OCR volumes and six Books renewal transcriptions. Queries included `Psalter`, `Reformed`, `Kuiper`, `Westra`, `Publication Committee`, and `A80029` with whitespace variations. This was an OCR/transcription search, not page-by-page inspection or certified clearance:

- Music: [1961 first half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig3155lib), [1961 second half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig3155libr), [1962 first half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig3165lib), [1962 second half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig3165libr), [1963 first half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig3175lib), [1963 second half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig3175libr)
- Books renewal transcriptions: [11823](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11823), [11824](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11824), [11825](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11825), [11826](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11826), [11827](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11827), [11828](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11828)

There is also affirmative contemporary evidence: in [his January 15, 2017 revision article](https://sb.rfpa.org/psalter-revision-governing-principles-1-format/), Psalter Revision Committee member Douglas J. Kuiper describes the existing Psalter's music as public domain while discussing the editions ending at 434 and 450. This is material evidence against presuming that the inherited music must be removed. It is a statement about music, not a separate clearance determination for every lyric, later edition, or jurisdiction.

### Nos. 433–434 and remaining narrow checks

For John A. Heys's 1947 settings, **EP17230** and **EP17231**, the prior recorded review found no matching renewal in the four Music catalog volumes: [1974 first half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig32852lib), [1974 second half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig32852libr), [1975 first half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig32952library), and [1975 second half](https://archive.org/details/catalogofcopyrig32952libr). That is supportive negative evidence, with search/OCR limits, rather than proof of a remaining right or a conclusive no-renewal determination. No actual current-rights basis requiring their removal has been established by this review.

Additional bounded checks of 1931 registrations, 1958–59 music/book renewals, and selected periodical catalogs also found no relevant component renewal. Exact first publication of every text remains unestablished; a 1931 author credit need not itself be a publication date. The later settings in 438–441 and 443–450 share the 1934 source-notice category, so that evidence is relevant to them too. A number above 434 does not itself establish a rights problem.

**No. 442's exact 1954 Henry Bruinsma harmony remains a separate unresolved question.** The 1934 search cannot decide it. A [1959 Psalter Hymnal registration, A393928](https://archive.org/details/CatalogOfCopyrightEntriesSeries3Vol.13Part5No.1jan.-june1959), records new material and names Bruinsma, but does not establish that this exact harmony first appeared there. An expanded search of the [Copyright Office renewal dataset](https://www.copyright.gov/economic-research/usco-datasets/) found no matching A393928 identifier or relevant Bruinsma/William Kuipers/CRC claimant record across 908,597 records. This was a bounded identifier/name search: 98.37% of records lacked a MARC 245 title field, and the Office cautions that the dataset is not a substitute for established records searches. It is not a complete title search or proof of non-renewal. The interactive catalog remained inaccessible. The [official 1955 Agenda, printed page 100](https://www.calvin.edu/library/database/crcnasynod/1955agenda.pdf#page=101) identifies a separate 1954 New Tune Supplement / Supplementary Agenda Report 28-A, page 42. No accessible public copy of that music page plus its cover/copyright page was located in the bounded search, so the exact harmony comparison remains unperformed. Exact first publication, component registration, and applicable renewal remain unresolved; no affirmative current-rights evidence requiring removal was established in this pass.

Apply evidence to the exact text/arrangement, not merely to a Psalter number or a familiar melody. These narrow questions do not justify converting all nos. 1–434 into presumptive blockers or automatically excluding any setting.

These findings are U.S.-focused and do not establish absolute worldwide clearance or cover unrelated recordings, modern revisions, scans, or artwork. Preserve [provenance](provenance.md), historical notices, and the evidence behind each conclusion. The retained corpus remains unchanged; these narrow evidence gaps remain explicitly qualified.

## Additional hymnbooks: no blanket clearance

The working added books share 97 source editions. Their source records retain differing licenses, source assertions, and qualifications. “Included” or “technically reviewed” describes the working application's selection; it is not a single legal status for the corpus.

Important recorded distinctions include:

- **The King of Love My Shepherd Is:** follow-up inspection confirms that the [actual source PDF](https://library.timelesstruths.org/library/music/T/The_King_of_Love_My_Shepherd_Is/The_King_of_Love_My_Shepherd_Is.pdf) agrees with the imported XML's 1912 altered-harmony/public-domain credit, while the HTML page says 1906. Both dates are historical. Preserve the metadata mismatch and [edition record](../src/data/hymns/editions/hymnEdition-tt-supplemental-the-king-of-love-my-shepherd-is.json), but do not present the discrepancy as an established U.S. rights blocker or evidence of a modern protected arrangement. The exact harmonizer remains unestablished.
- **FINLANDIA:** both [We Rest on Thee](../src/data/hymns/editions/hymnEdition-tt-supplemental-we-rest-on-thee.json) and [Be Still, My Soul](../src/data/hymns/editions/hymnEdition-oh_be-still-my-soul-finlandia.json) have an affirmative territorial qualification. [Teosto, which administers Sibelius rights](https://www.teosto.fi/en/teosto-manages-copyright-in-sibelius-works/), states that FINLANDIA is public domain in the United States but protected in Finland and most of Europe until expiry in 2027. This is not a U.S. removal requirement. Preserve the territorial distinction and any separate arrangement terms.
- **Other reviewed editions:** the historical Bittikofer edition of [Complete in Thee](../src/data/hymns/editions/hymnEdition-mobile-complete-in-thee.json) has an [actual source PDF marked public domain](https://www.mobilehymns.org/PDF/Complete_In_Thee_Bittikofer.pdf); it is not the modern Ben Nyce setting. [Hymn Commons expressly supports reuse of its transcriptions](https://hymncommons.org/about/), supporting the retained sources for [Turn Your Eyes Upon Jesus](../src/data/hymns/editions/hymnEdition-hc-turn-your-eyes.json) and [Wonderful Grace of Jesus](../src/data/hymns/editions/hymnEdition-hc-wonderful-grace.json). The follow-up established no U.S. exclusion requirement for these exact editions. Underlying-work rights in other countries still require attention; these source statements do not supply worldwide rights the source does not hold.
- **20 Timeless Truths editions:** their acquisition records retain the disclaimer that acquisition did not clear distribution, alongside source public-domain assertions and original caveats. These disclaimers are not automatically formal holds or proof that all 20 remain copyrighted. Preserve them as research limits alongside any newer edition-level evidence; neither they nor the King of Love metadata mismatch automatically requires exclusion.
- **Ten OpenPsalm arrangements:** these are licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), not blanket public-domain arrangements. Retain the credited creators, source and license links, and the indication of ABC conversion/normalization or other changes. Underlying-work and edition scope remain relevant.
- **Anthology material and other sources:** retain the exact scope of CC0 or other source grants and their underlying-work qualifications. A waiver for new anthology contributions does not resolve every underlying work or unrelated later edition.
- **I Have Decided to Follow Jesus:** remains excluded pending exact-edition rights evidence. Preserve the [existing hold record](../src/data/hymns/source/RIGHTS_HOLD.json); neither a source's public-domain footer nor the presence of a historical copyright notice alone settles the unresolved question.

Use the [edition records](../src/data/hymns/editions/), [source credits and reuse conditions](../src/data/hymns/source/CREDITS_AND_REUSE.txt), and [hymn data guide](../src/data/hymns/README.md) when making an exact selection. The newer findings refine the interpretation of earlier warnings without altering source records or clearing the added hymn corpus as a whole.

## What must happen before release

The original code/documentation MIT choice is complete. This source retains the full working corpus with the qualifications above. A redistributor must evaluate the exact editions and intended territory. Resolve the relevant remaining evidence questions before deciding whether any edition needs permission, a different edition, or exclusion. Source-export and live-app decisions must each account for how they distribute content; omission from one does not settle the other. This source removes no music and grants no blanket content rights. Keep all required attribution and existing licenses attached to retained material.

Follow the [public-source guide](public-release.md), then validate the exact exported source and built assets. Tests and `validate:rights-audit` check technical consistency; they do not provide rights clearance or replace the edition-specific distribution review.
